# Case study: Reworking Marketplace documentation around the customer journey

**Role:** Content developer and cross-functional documentation partner  
**Timing:** Fall 2023  
**Focus:** Customer experience, information architecture, stakeholder enablement, and delivery coordination

[Explore the current Marketplace customer documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/marketplace/)

## The challenge

Microsoft’s Marketplace customer guidance needed a coordinated refresh. The marketing and product teams wanted documentation that better helped customers understand and navigate Marketplace transactions—and gave field sellers clearer resources to use when supporting them.

The work required more than revising individual articles. We needed to improve how customers entered the documentation, how topics were organized, and how marketing, engineering, and documentation contributors worked together to deliver the changes.

## My contribution

I partnered with Marketplace marketing, product engineering, and documentation colleagues on a 3-month revamp ahead of an October Marketplace Summit.

I worked closely with marketing and product-management consultants and the product group to implement article revisions, create a scenario-based table of contents, and build a customer-oriented entry experience.

My contribution combined hands-on content work with helping stakeholders navigate the publishing process. I provided guidance on documentation requirements, clarified the steps needed to move changes forward, and helped the teams make consistent progress within a compressed timeframe.

I also helped stakeholders become more self-sufficient. My aim was to provide the direction and support they needed while enabling them to contribute through the available authoring tools.

## What changed

The project delivered a revised documentation experience with updated articles, scenario-based navigation, and a new entry page.

The experience was showcased at the October Marketplace Summit. Stakeholder feedback emphasized both the improvement in the documentation and the value of having a direct, responsive documentation partner who could connect marketing and engineering work.

The results were collaborative. I contributed documentation expertise, implementation, structure, and coordination; marketing and product partners supplied their domain knowledge and business priorities.

### We made customer scenarios easier to find

The revamp replaced broad entry-point groupings with more explicit customer scenarios. The revised experience surfaced paths for finding solutions, navigating private offers, purchasing, managing billing and costs, and applying governance controls. Working with marketing and product partners, I helped translate those needs into revised articles, scenario-based navigation, and a reorganized entry page.

## What I learned

The project also sharpened my judgment about balancing stakeholder requests with platform standards.

We implemented a hub page with product-group approval, but I later recognized that a landing page would have better matched the documentation platform’s information-architecture guidance for this content set. A more prominent entry experience also needed careful link prioritization rather than exposing too many destinations at once.

My takeaway was to investigate the reasoning behind an established constraint early, distinguish product-group approval from platform guidance, and resolve those differences before a deadline creates pressure to proceed.

That lesson complements the delivery outcome: successful collaboration includes helping stakeholders understand the tradeoffs in the experience they request.

## What this demonstrates

- Translating customer and business needs into documentation structure.
- Coordinating delivery across marketing, engineering, and content teams.
- Combining hands-on revisions with stakeholder guidance and enablement.
- Reflecting critically on design decisions and improving subsequent judgment.
